## 109. DAW rules for the selection, and the first gesture goes on a key

J opened the day with three asks in one message: "Can you organize
the cursor and selection behavior better? so I can make a
selection, but then the cursor is still there. And if I click off
somewhere, the selection is still there ... the standard behavior
you find in [a DAW]. Let's make the set playing keys in different
speeds also respond to the selection. So if selection, then play
the selection. If just cursor, then play the set. Then can we
build a sequence? 5, 3, 16, 18 in that order each will come in
after one repeat of the previous, the individual set behavior
will be loop each repeat increase 20% speed lets stop after the
last one has had 4 loops; assign to available key please"

Landed, in order:

- **Selection/cursor coexistence** was already the built behavior
  (drag never moves the cursor; a click never clears the
  selection; ESC clears) — what was missing was the priority
  rule, now in: **a live selection is the unit** — every speed
  key (digits, the W-row, SHIFT variants) plays the selection
  once at that speed and parks at its head; with no selection,
  the set under the cursor, as before.
- **The sequence** is the gesture library's first real entry,
  built as page-side machinery (startSeq): voices in canon —
  set 5 (suspended wash, 2.55 s) · 3 (smeared, 2.72 s) · 16
  (wash, 2.86 s) · 18 (jpverb dark, 2.47 s) — each entering when
  its predecessor completes one loop pass, every voice looping
  its own set and growing 20% per pass (capped at 16x), the
  whole texture ending the moment the last voice finishes its
  4th loop, all voices fading out and unspawning. Ephemeral
  voices, an explicit end state, entry stagger, growth-per-
  repeat: four of the behavior-spec's ideas in one motive.
- **On Q** (the free key left of the W-row): Q starts it, Q
  again — or ESC, or a stop — tears it down. Chat verbs seq/
  seqstop take overrides ({sets, grow, lastLoops}), so variants
  cost a sentence.

Verified with a fast config (sets [5,3], lastLoops 2) sampled
live: voice 1 looped set 5 at 1.0, wrapped, bumped to 1.2 exactly
as voice 2 entered at set 3; both grew; the last voice's second
pass ended the gesture and teardown left one playhead. Selection
priority measured the same way: a ~2 s drag + digit 2 ran at
tempo 2.0 inside the selection bounds and parked at sel.t0.
Zero console errors. J's full 4-set config runs on the identical
code path.

Reading recorded for his correction if wrong: "each will come in
after one repeat of the previous" built as LAYERED entries — the
earlier voices keep looping and accelerating under the new ones
(the accretion shape), not a relay where each stops as the next
starts.

## 110. The envelopes level up

Mid-build, J: "Did you already give a default gain boost? to the
change speed ones, but also the envelope ones are quiet too."
The first half: yes — the +12 dB granular makeup has been the
default since §107 (it needed his reload; the overnight server
death muddied who was hearing what). The second half was real
and unfixed: envelope plays carried peak 0.9, but a shape's
loudness is its RMS, and the Roads shapes' RMS spans 0.2
(expodec, rexpodec) to 0.85 (trapezoid) — so the peaky shapes
sat ~10 dB under the fat ones, and all of them under raw
playback.

Fix: **every envelope is now levelled to hann's RMS** (0.612 of
peak) — shapes differ in contour, not loudness — with the peak
boost capped at 2.2 and a global dial (verb envgain{db}) on top
for taste. Measured at one spot (91.5, 300 ms, master RMS
peaks): hann 0.0172 · expodec 0.0189 · gaussian 0.0187 · sinc
0.0242 — within ±1.5 dB of each other where expodec had been
~x3 down in energy. The equal-loudness principle is the same as
§107's: what varies between candidates should be the thing
under audition, never the volume.

## 112. The canon's false wraps, and the C key

J on Q: "Q sequence not right. I don't know if it's a bug or
misunderstanding" — then dictated the intended timeline in full
(five alone at normal speed; its second pass 20% faster
coinciding with three's entry; every voice looping independently,
each repeat 20% up; each next set entering as its predecessor
begins repeat two; the whole thing ending after the last set's
repeats — raised from four to "actually eight." And a future
note: "I could [set] the end time variable and assign total
duration time regardless of where anyone is in the repeat
cycle.").

The timeline instrument settled bug-vs-misunderstanding in one
run: it was a bug, and a race. A newborn voice's mirrored t
reads 0 until its seek lands in the worklet (~10-30 ms); the
wrap detector saw "t collapsed" — a false wrap — bumped the
newborn instantly and, since entries key on the predecessor's
pass count, cascaded the next entry: the measured log showed 3
AND 16 both entering within 0.11 s ("five played once alone"
never happened) while 16, winning the same race by luck,
behaved. Non-deterministic, hence "I don't know if it's a bug."
Fix: a voice's wrap detection stays disarmed until the worklet
reports a position inside its own loop; out-of-range reports are
ignored.

The corrected timeline, measured: 5 alone at 1.0 · 2.37 s: 5 →
1.2 as 3 enters at 1.0 · 5.13 s: 3 → 1.2 as 16 enters · 7.98 s:
16 → 1.2 as 18 enters · every ladder ×1.2 at its own wraps
only. Matches his dictation event for event. lastLoops default
now 8. Flagged to him honestly from the same log: at 20% per
repeat, geometric growth runs away — set 5 passes 15x by ~13 s
and will sit at the 16x ceiling for the back half of an 8-loop
run. That is the spec's own arithmetic; his end-time-variable
idea is the natural governor, parked at his word ("but let me
hear the whole thing").

And a workflow key, from his mid-turn ask ("I need to get the

## 125. Goldilocks gaps, and the seed makes patterns durable

The refinement came within minutes of hearing the first rolls:

Built exactly so: gap classes short 0.05-0.2 s / medium
