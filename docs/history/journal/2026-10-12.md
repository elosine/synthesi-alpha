**What was built.**

1. **The tremolo sits on the cursors' bus** (engine/voices.js
   `busOverlay`): the bus's gain moved by one LFO — an oscillator of
   the browser's own, its speed steady or scheduled to move — over
   everything the cursors play. Electronics main is not on that bus.
   This is the second KIND of overlay, and the two kinds are his
   distinction of §218 made into code: *on each hit* (the crescendo)
   and *over everything* (the tremolo).
2. **Five candidates** in the scenario file's `"overlays"`:

   | name | shape | depth | speed |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `trem` | sine | 24 dB | 8 Hz, steady |
   | `trem accel` | sine | 24 dB | 4 → 24 Hz over 8 s, and begin again |
   | `trem rush` | sine | 24 dB | 4 → 24 Hz over 3 s, and begin again |
   | `trem waves` | sine | 24 dB | 4 → 24 Hz over 8 s and back down over 8 |
   | `chop accel` | a square with its corners off | to silence | 4 → 24 Hz over 8 s, and begin again |

3. **On the play-in page** the row is off · cresc · trem · trem accel ·
   trem rush · trem waves · chop accel. A tremolo runs from the moment
   its button is pressed. A recording begins it AGAIN at its first hit,
   and keeps every switch as an event of its own (`kind: "bus"`), so a
   take replays with the tremolo where it was.
4. **A stored pattern played while a button is on is laid under that
   overlay**; with "off" it plays as it was played in. (New, and it
   holds for `cresc` too: it is how his existing sequences can be heard
   under an overlay without playing them in again — §208: "in some
   cases I might not need to, and we'll just overlay the effects on top
   of it.")
5. **From START** a page's `"overlay"` takes the bus when the page's
   section begins — at its stop — and runs from the top of its pattern;
   a page that names none clears the bus.

**The screen** (`python tools/overlay_levels.py --screen`; a steady
sine tremolo 24 dB deep, on all 67 phrases — median 0.82 s — and all
69 attacks, 0.35 s each).

| speed | turns in an attack | in a phrase | two turns fit in | the lottery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Hz | 0.7 | 1.6 | 19 % of hits | 8.6 dB |
| 4 Hz | 1.4 | 3.3 | 39 % | 7.6 dB |
| 6 Hz | 2.1 | 4.9 | 100 % | 7.0 dB |
| 8 Hz | 2.8 | 6.6 | 100 % | 6.6 dB |
| 16 Hz | 5.6 | 13.1 | 100 % | 6.0 dB |
| 24 Hz | 8.4 | 19.7 | 100 % | 5.4 dB |
| 32 Hz | 11.2 | 26.3 | 100 % | 4.2 dB |

- **Six hertz is a floor.** Under it the LFO does not turn twice inside
  an attack: it is heard ACROSS the hits — this one loud, that one soft
  — and not inside them. From 6 Hz up every hit holds at least two
  turns.
- **The lottery does not go away.** "The lottery" is how far a hit's
  loudest 50 ms moves with where the hit happens to land on the wave
  (5th to 95th percentile, 16 phases). It is 8.6 dB at 2 Hz and still
  5.4 dB at 24 Hz — well over the 2–3 dB JND at every speed tried. The
  reason is the material: these hits are attacks first, and an attack
  lands on ONE point of the wave however fast the wave turns. So a
  tremolo over these sequences is always two things at once: a flutter
  inside the phrases, and a re-dealing of which hits speak.
- **How fast it quickens decides where the quickening is heard.**
  4 → 24 Hz over 8 s changes the speed by about a quarter inside one
  phrase — at the edge of the JND for a time (20 %): the acceleration
  is heard from hit to hit. Over 3 s it is three-quarters inside one
  phrase: heard INSIDE the hit. Those are `trem accel` and `trem rush`.
- *Set aside:* other depths (a shallower shimmer — a tweak, less / as
  is / more, once a kind is chosen) · the triangle (built, unoffered) ·
  a steady fast flutter (20 Hz and up; it is where `accel` arrives
  anyway) · anything under 4 Hz steady (the floor).

**One loudness.** A tremolo takes power out; its makeup puts the same
power back (the mean of gain² over a turn brought to 1: +4.1 dB for
the sine at 24 dB, +3.0 for the chop). His first sequence, pt1 (27
hits, 19.5 s), was recorded from the page as played and under each
candidate, and the takes measured over their sounding 20.3 s
(tools/take_levels.py): **trem −0.4 dB · trem accel −0.1 · trem rush

*Told to him:* the makeup lifts the crests — the takes' peaks went from
−4.4 dBFS to between −1.2 and −3.0, up to the engine's limiter at −1.

**That it is in the sound** (the takes' envelopes, since the AI cannot
hear): under `trem` the share of the envelope's movement lying at
7–9 Hz is 7 dB up on the untouched take (−5.6 against −12.7 dB); under
`trem accel` the envelope's strongest speed, second by second, climbs

**The other checks.**

### B1 — make a version

**What it is.** `perform.html?make` — [begin], ENTER — plays the piece
from START and KEEPS it. `rig.scenario.makeVersion()` for the AI's
pane. A version is a folder, `build/versions/seed-N/`:

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `sound.flac` | the piece — 48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo, packed lossless |
| `picture.json` | where electronics main was, and every sound that was called |
| `version.json` | the seed, the length, the levels, the check, the commit that made it |
| `scenario.json` | the scenario file as it stood |

`build/` is not in git: these are candidates (S5). A folder that
already holds a version is not written over unless told to.

**How the sound is taken.** A small processor in the audio thread
(engine/version-recorder.js) sits on the engine's output after the
limiter — what is heard, before the page's own mute and mono switch —
and keeps every sample, 32-bit float, handing the page a second at a
time. The page posts each second to the repo server in order
(engine/version.js → tools/serve.py `/version-…`). Every block says
which sample it begins on; the page counts what was lost between two
of them. At the end the server brings the float sound to 16-bit with
triangular dither and packs it (ffmpeg, FLAC level 8).

*Rejected:* the recorder the page already had (`rig.scenario.record`)
— `MediaRecorder`, webm/Opus, compressed; made for clips. *Rejected:*
rendering faster than real time (an offline context) — the scenario
runs on timers and messages; it would be a rewrite of the piece to
save ten minutes a version.

**How the picture is kept — not as pixels.** The record is what the
page draws FROM: electronics main as a line a run (it leaves score
time p0 at c0, is on p1 at c1, and stays); and each sound — its kind,
its place in the score, when it began and ended, its colour, forward
or reversed, and for a read that does not move evenly (a moving icy)
33 points of how it moved.

**A thing found that had to be fixed first: the waits were timed by
a timer.** Each page's stand was a `setTimeout`. On a page that is
not shown — another tab in front, the window covered, the AI's pane —
a browser holds timers to one a second, and each page would stand up
to a second too long. A ten-minute recording pass WILL be left in the
background. So a run now begins on the AUDIO clock: at the moment of a
stop the next start is handed to the worklet for "this stop + the
wait", and the worklet begins it at that sample-block. No timer stands
between a stop and the next start.

That touched the final draft's engine — said plainly, three small
ways:

1. *A start that waits its frame* (engine/playhead-processor.js,
   playhead-engine.js): the same gate-on as before, held in the
   worklet's queue until its moment; it reports when it began.
2. *The reverb's send closes 0.3 s after a stop on the audio clock*,
   where a timer closed it (same moment on a shown page).
3. *The next run is known to the page from the stop before it.* A
   sound scheduled across a run's start is now judged on-screen or off
   against the score as it WILL be moving, where before it was judged
   as if main were still. A corner; and the truer reading of "if they
   go off the screen, they go off the screen".
