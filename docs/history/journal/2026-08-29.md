## 53. The glow around the cursor: the plume was never alive until today

J, at the full score: "Everything has a gray glow around the cursor now...
I don't know how that snuck in. The preview didn't have that." He asked
for an assessment before any change - nothing touched yet.

Diagnosis:

- The glow is the **plume** from motion pass 2 (§45) - pl0/pl1, "the
  stingray's cloud, the dust between grains."
- Its gradient offsets were emitted as literal `0%%`/`55%%`/`100%%` -
  strings that never pass through %-formatting. The browser rejected all
  three offsets, defaulted them to 0, and the ellipses painted at the last
  stop's opacity: **zero**. The plume has been invisible in every build J
  calibrated - passes 2 and 3 were tuned without it on screen.
- This morning's fix (§51) resurrected it. Not new code; old intent
  rendering for the first time. Correction to §51, which read as a pure
  restoration ("the dust now actually fades to nothing"): what the fix
  actually did was surface a feature the calibrated look never contained.
- The blink: size and opacity are slaved, unsmoothed, to a 20 ms loudness
  envelope, with a 0.10/0.06 opacity floor and two stacked ellipses.

What it tracks (J asked "what in the sound is the plume tracking? If
anything"): **the instantaneous loudness of the full mix** - mono sum,
RMS over 20 ms windows (50/s), normalized so the 95th-percentile moment
= 1.0. pl0 is that value at the cursor's instant; pl1 is the same value
0.45 s ago, a wake. Nothing structural - not a layer, not wetness, not
density. And the information is already in the score: **bed thickness is
the same envelope** read at each grain's moment (rest = (4 + le^0.8 x 34)
x frac). The plume re-states as a glow what the bed already states as
thickness.

## 66. The rings learn where the rocks are, and pulse with the sound

J's report on the first ring trial, with a screenshot: "the ripples
don't seem to emanate from the rocks, and somehow it doesn't really
sync with the sound. I'm not sure why for either of those, if you can
figure that out."

Diagnosis of both, from the code:

- **Not from the rocks**: the rings were anchored at each slab's
  *resting* place in the bed, while the slab was high in its flight
  when the repeats fired. Invisible in the Lab (small kicks), glaring
  in the score (kicks to ~190 px).
- **No sync**: every grain emitted two rings on its own private clock -
  in a real section that is hundreds of unsynchronized rings in ~2.6 s.
  The Lab had six separated slabs; the score made ring-fog, and fog
  cannot sync with anything. The lab-to-score scale jump is itself the
  lesson: a motion that reads at n=6 can dissolve at n=300.

Mid-fix, J respec'd it better than the fix: "There are clear echo
repeats. And so maybe we sync the ripples with those echo repeats, so
all of them, pulse, and each repeat. and then we just reorient the
spatial ripples so they look like they're emanating from each of the
rocks. regardless of where they are vertically. they're tracked with
the rocks themselves."

Built exactly so: every plates grain carries its section's measured
onset as an anchor; all rings pulse on one **0.28 s grid** from that
anchor (the two ring elements alternate pulses, 0.56 s life each); a
ring's centre is its slab's position *now*, so it rides the flight;
rings run from each slab's attack for 1.6 s, fading. My interim
40%-thinning gate was dropped - "all of them, pulse" - because
synchronized rings are a coherent wave, and it was the phase chaos,
not the count, that made fog. Verified in stills: halos riding slabs.
The unison pulse is J's to judge by ear.

his mark. What the data says:

- The container opens at 42.44; the old rule puts sound at 43.108. But
  43.1-43.8 is *quiet transitional material* - levels ~0.02-0.03, the
  diffused tail dying out.
- The section's first strong compound attack is exactly where he
  pointed: a loud gran pair at 43.859/43.863 (amp 0.284/0.243), **a

  44.002 (+0.068 to level 0.090, 3.5x the level of everything since
  43.1).
- So the ear's rule, in data terms: **the section starts at its first
  characteristic attack, not at the container edge and not at the
  first faint sound.** A classifier candidate falls out directly:
  onset = first attack-cluster after the container opens whose level
  jump exceeds a threshold scaled to the section's own peak. J's ear
  marks (88.65 boulders, 27.32-era plates, 43.93 here) become the
  ground truth that fits the threshold. That is the expedite he
  suspected.
- Honest negative, recorded: the 0.28 s echo periodicity is NOT
  measurable in the mix RMS inside this section (sliding lag-0.28

  J hears are real but not simple envelope periodicity - so the ring
  pulse grid keeps the transcribed 0.28 s for now, and if it fights

## 104. "Takes me several clicks": the lens was eating the seek

J: "Curser is hard to move. Takes me several clicks." The bug was
latent in the performance page since the spill build (§94), and
the sandbox's first real use surfaced it: view-2 click-seek
required the event target to be inside a .lane, but each
playhead's LENS — the live-animation window floating above the
lanes, spanning [-6 s, +2 s] around the cursor — captured every
click near the cursor, which is precisely where a hand aims when
nudging it. Clicks far away worked; clicks where they matter
died. Several clicks = hunting for a spot the lens didn't cover.

Two fixes, both in perform.html core (the performance page
inherits them): .lens gets pointer-events:none (it is display
only), and the seek handler finds its lane by coordinates instead
of by event target, so nothing that ever floats above the lanes
can eat a seek again. Verified with hit-testing and real clicks:
elementFromPoint returns the lane raster at on-lens and off-lens
points alike; two clicks landed at 136.83 and 90.12 — the exact
lane-math times for their pixels. (A false alarm during
verification, worth keeping: reads taken in the same tool batch
as the click reported the cursor unmoved — the read raced the
event queue. The state a few hundred ms later told the truth.
Measure after the dust, not during.)

## 107. Why SPACE is loud: coherence, measured — and the makeup dial

J, minutes into the keys: "All the keyboard plays, like,
different speeds or one of the envelopes are much quieter. Do we
know the reason for this? Spacebar plays much louder."

We do now, with numbers. Same material (seek 97.0, identical
sample window), worklet RMS:

- tempo 1.00 (SPACE): 0.1225 — reference
- tempo 1.01: 0.0011 — **-41 dB**
- tempo 1.50: 0.0104 — -21.4 dB
- tempo 0.50: 0.0184 — -16.5 dB

The 1.01 row is the crux: material identical, only alignment
changed. At tempo == pitch the 8 overlapped taps read the same
aligned signal and reconstruct it coherently at full level; at
any other tempo they slide apart and partially cancel. SPACE is
the one coherent case of the §5b decomposition — every stretch,
freeze, and backward lives in the quiet regime. Inherent to
granular time-stretch (Warp1 does it too), not a bug. The
envelope keys are a second, smaller effect: raw-buffer playback
at peak 0.9, but an envelope's average gain is a fraction of its
peak by design.

J: "a and then b later" — fixed makeup now, auto-makeup (output
levelled against the material's own loudness) later. Built into
the worklet: a `makeup` param (default +12 dB) scaled by
misalignment min(1, |tempo - pitch|/0.03), so coherent playback
is mathematically untouched and the boost is fully in by 3% off.
Engine exposes makeupDb; sandbox verb `makeup {db}` dials it
live, all playheads or one.

Verified through the reloaded worklet: tempo 1.0 unchanged
(0.1244 ≈ noise of 0.1225); 1.5x rose 0.0104 → 0.0389 (+11.5 of
the +12) and 0.5x rose 0.0184 → 0.0742 (+12.1); dialing 18 dB
live mid-run pulled 1.5x to -3.3 dB from SPACE. Note kept
honest: a flat boost cannot chase material — the pathological
tonal spots (the -41 dB comb at 1.01) stay quieter than SPACE
until (b), the AGC against the material envelope, lands.
