## 40. The workflow codified: briefings, the Choosing Lab, and the spec

J's requirement, distilled from a long exchange: a repeatable by-ear process
where every stage opens with a plain explanation of what is being decided,
candidates are auditioned rather than parameters chosen, and the decided thing
is stored so the folder tells the build story.

The pieces built:

- **The spec** (`procspec.py`) - one JSON holding time containers, effects and
  control *separately*, because he wants independent say over each: "the tc
  should be different, but in a general way", "no freeze but add distortion",
  "mostly reverb and 20% of the time other fx", "repeat the fx in order". Modes:
  state-driven, weighted, fixed order; exclusions in all of them. Portable: the
  same spec over a different bed re-establishes the sequence.
- **The strip** (`strip_svg.py`) - wrapped 30-second rows; colours *derived*
  from the families' own two axes so similar colours mean similar sounds;
  sparklines under each row showing cutoff and reverb time moving - the half of
  the system blocks cannot show. His request, held from earlier in the day.
- **The Choosing Lab** - its own artifact, current round only, briefing at the
  top. Rejected candidates' parameters go here, not the page.
- **Voice panels** - one artifact per voice, decided phases only. Temporary
  scaffolding by his own description: he is learning the process and wants to
  see how a voice was made.

Round 1 is live for v-alpha's processing stage: three deliberately cornered
candidates over a 45 s slice of the bed -

    lab-a  smeared and slow    14 containers   suspended 20s, rung 10s
    lab-b  a fixed cycle       15 containers   spaced/rung/suspended/detuned
    lab-c  fast alternation    32 containers   scattered 12s, ruined 11s

Also today, at his request: the standing end-every-reply listening-room link is
retired, and stage briefings are to be in my clearest words rather than his
dictated examples, which were examples.

## 43. A score for v-alpha: the mockup, and what the first drawing taught

> I just want a static representation of the v alpha that maybe eventually
> I'll use as some kind of notation, and then just have a timeline and scroll
> vertical line across it with playback.

J's design, dictated: grayscale; amplitude bipolar around a midline ("when
things explode, they go up and down"); grains at their exact times; dry grains
black, washy ones grayer; circle versus amoeba; static score with a scrolling
cursor - he raised and himself retired the idea of full animation, keeping
static because it is to serve as a score.

My additions, accepted in advance: filled marks for grains that generate, rings
for grains that read from the material; amoeba-ness mapped to the surface-damage
axis the families already carry; the mix's amplitude silhouette as a faint
background; container boundaries as midline ticks; pan side deciding up or down.

**The event lists are finally kept.** Every drawn mark is a real event: the
6,095 grain records (time, kind, duration, amplitude, pan) extracted from the
render scores and written into the voice folder as tracked JSON - closing the
"exact events are not preserved" item that had been open since the taxonomy
work.

### What the first drawing taught

**J's amplitude rule produces bands, not explosions.** Grain amplitudes are
deliberately near-uniform - the 1/sqrt(overlap) compensation exists precisely
to keep them level - so displacement-by-amplitude draws four ruled stripes.
The mockup shows his rule (A) and a variant (B) where grains ride the mix's
local loudness, side by side over the same audio and cursor.

And B failed first too, invisibly: normalising the envelope to the global peak
put most of the piece near zero and the whole cloud hugged the midline.
Normalised to the 95th percentile instead - one loud moment must not flatten
every other one.

- The mockup: 30 s from 1:22, the densest stretch, 1,265 marks, click-to-seek
  cursor. https://claude.ai/code/artifact/2faa7dab-b368-4eb4-89f5-04bdf4d514aa

## 44. The sand score

J revised the score after seeing the two static variants, and the revision
dissolved the problem entry 43 found. His model:

> the scrolling cursor will disturb. So grains should stay in place, like
> animations of sound pressure waves. The molecules don't move through the
> air. They're just displaced in place. [...] there's some sort of gravity.
> They descend at a certain pace. They descend back into their bed.

Decided: rest state a1 (a bed compressed toward the midline whose thickness
follows the local loudness, so the static page still reads as a score) and
settle b (wetness-dependent - dry sand drops in ~0.35 s, reverb-soaked grains
float for ~3 s, so **the visual tail is the reverb tail**).

The amplitude rule that banded as a static position lives naturally as motion:
kick height is the grain's own amplitude. A transient is where per-grain
loudness actually is.

Also his: grains as sand under a microscope - jagged, rock-like, always
filled, sizes halved so the old median is the new maximum. The ring-vs-filled
distinction retired.

### No physics engine

Displacement is a pure function of (now - the grain's true time): 60 ms rise,
then a gravity curve over the wetness-dependent settle. No simulation state, so
seeking anywhere renders the instantly correct picture, and 1,265 grains
animate at frame rate without effort. The grain is disturbed at the exact
moment it sounds, which is not a metaphor but the fact of the matter.

Verified before showing: at rest all 1,265 grains lie in a 75-pixel bed; a
seek to 15 s displaces exactly the 90 grains inside their settle windows,
spreading the cloud to 213 pixels.

- The score: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/2faa7dab-b368-4eb4-89f5-04bdf4d514aa

## 45. Motion pass two: clusters, trails, plume, and reverb as material

J's refinements after living with the sand score, talked through before
building:

- **Each sound grain became a small cluster of rocks** - his own suggestion,
  refined from a "clone" brainstorm: one main rock and two tucked satellites
  that take different kicks and drift sideways in flight, so the cloud fills
  vertically and billows horizontally while every sound grain keeps a visual
  identity. At rest the cluster reads as one grain, so the static bed he
  likes is unchanged.
- **Trails** ("that might just draw some lines" - literally so: a fading
  streak from bed to rock) and the **stingray plume** - a soft dust swell
  behind the disturbance, first built as two hard ellipses that read as rings,
  fixed with a radial gradient.
- **Velocity from attack**: loud grains peak in ~25 ms, soft in ~120.
- **Depth** by seeded near/far scaling in flight.
- **The reverb tail moved out of time into the material.** Position now
  settles fast for everyone (0.2-0.8 s), keeping the rhythm tight; wetness
  makes a grain land swollen and pale, and the time its shape takes to harden
  again is the tail. His formulation, kept: "if we fill the shape in, we can
  adjust the time that it settles back to its original shape."

The static score stays as it is, deliberately. He considered outline overlays
for what-comes-next and rejected them - "the outline would disturb its current
visual elegance" - and noted that real performer notation, if it comes, will be
a different artefact with different obligations.

3,795 rocks now animate from the same pure function of (now - grain time);
verified 213 in flight at a test seek before showing.

## 46. Motion pass three: the filled body, and giving the float back

Two corrections from J's listening, one of them to a change he had asked for:

**The trails read as lines, not sand.** Removed entirely.

**Pass two killed the 19-second moment.** He had asked for faster decay, pass
two obliged by settling everyone fast and moving all of wetness into
deformation - and that erased the thing he had singled out as working: frozen
grains floating gray, then the dry section turning suddenly vigorous. The
lesson is worth keeping at full weight: **a requested change can destroy the
requested feeling**, and only listening catches it. Settle time is
wetness-dependent again (dry ~0.25 s, frozen ~2.5 s), with pass two's swell
and pallor kept on top - so the float and the deformation now reinforce each
other instead of one replacing the other.

**The cloud became a filled body.** His traditional-animation image: settled
shape, twenty-percent shape, eighty, hundred - each still full of grains. The
mechanism: every rock owns a fixed *fraction* of its cluster's height, so at
any expansion the column from bed to tip stays packed at the bed's own
density; nothing hollows out. And the rocks boil internally while excited
(seeded sine wobble, still a pure function of time), so the body has motion
inside it rather than being an outline that grows.

Verified before showing: no trail elements remain; just after the frozen
region 366 rocks hang displaced against 183 at a comparable dry moment - the
float carries twice the airborne population, which is the 19-second effect,
restored and measurable.

Next, agreed but not built: substrate textures per section (the grindy
boulders at ~6 s - which the data says is suspended/wash smearing dense
grain material, not Greyhole), and sustained tones detected from the audio as
their own ribbon species, glowing under the cursor.

## 47. Score versus visualizer, in J's own sorting

After a day inside the sand score, J separated its two functions, and the
separation is worth keeping verbatim because it will govern what gets built
next:

> the score aspects of it - my instinct is just to represent the static part,
> so you can see in advance and get a sense of what that texture might be
> like. [...] the differences in animation give me visual interest, but don't
> necessarily accomplish any notational functions. Which is not necessarily
> wrong. Just trying to clarify.

So: **the static page is the score** (see ahead - the freeze gray, the
substrate textures), and **the animation is the visualizer** (a performance
presentation - "if I want to show the score, which I probably do"). Two
functions, one artefact, different obligations. He also dropped his earlier
idea of molding the static shape by amplitude: "maybe I don't need to
represent amplitude in the actual static shape."

Decisions landed today along the way:

- Grayscale became an **applied effect marker** - freeze wears gray 160/150,
  everything else black - replacing the measured-wetness gray. Then texture:
  22% of black-field grains take seeded grays up to 100, a clear step below
  the freeze marker, meaning nothing but material.
- Line lag calibrated by J with a live slider: **0.30 s**.
- The boulders substrate is back in, now on the marker palette, and sits
  exactly where the sound grinds: the wash container at 5.8-8.5 s of the
  slice. The precision is not insight but bookkeeping - the containers are
  re-derived deterministically from the spec, so the score knows the change
  points because it computes the same ones the renderer did.

And the day's expensive lesson, recorded in the commits as it happened: every
"the animation is late" report traced to a missing width attribute - the SVG
scaled to the window while the cursor moved in CSS pixels, two clocks whose
gap grew. The lead chase (0.12, a one-second proof, 0.75, a lagged line) was
all downstream of it, and the lag slider that finally settled the question was
itself dead on arrival for one round because the variable it set had been lost
in a rewrite. Verify the wiring before calibrating the value.

## 48. The substrate is iconic, not literal - J's philosophy note

Kept in his words, because it settles what the score function is:

> what's working is the substrate in the static score marking when there's a
> notable change in texture. And it's more symbolic or icon-like, in that I
> don't need a one-to-one with the effects or the sound - but you can look
> ahead and see that the sound will change when the substrate changes, and it
> gives a general feel of what that sound will sound like, but it doesn't have
> to fully represent the upcoming sound.

So the substrate is a **sign, not a transcription**. The freeze gray works the
same way, and he pinned colour as a possible extension of the same principle -
"marking things in the static score somehow when the texture changes more
dramatically."

  millisecond marks rather than the container dials - the ear's timing worn
